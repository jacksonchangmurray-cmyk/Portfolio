    Center of pressure

    Where the air's push on the rocket balances out, worked out from your fins and nose cone (Barrowman method). It's the orange ring on the model.

    Balance the finished rocket (motor in) on your finger and measure from the tip. A stable rocket has its center of gravity at least one body width (100 mm) ahead of the center of pressure.

    How it works: a lattice Boltzmann fluid solver (with a Smagorinsky turbulence model) runs live on a 2D slice through the rocket's centerline, using its real outline from the STEP file. Drag and lift coefficients come from the force of the air on that outline. To run in real time it simulates thicker air than real flight (a lower Reynolds number), so treat the numbers as trends; the flow patterns (stagnation at the nose, separation and vortex shedding behind the base) are the real thing.

    Converted from the original STEP file with OpenCascade. Rendered with three.js.
